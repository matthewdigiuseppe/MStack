# Reviewer pools by journal family

Read by `/mstack:referee-mock` to draw a five-reviewer panel. Who referees a
paper is set by where it goes: editors invite from the journal's own author
base, its editorial board, and the authors the manuscript cites. The sketches
below are starting pools by family of journal. Check each against the
journal's current editors, board, and last two years of articles before
profiling: editorial teams turn over every few years and the pool moves with
them.

## How editors draw

- **Cited authors first.** The most common invitation goes to the author of a
  Frontier paper the manuscript engages, so the insider slot is nearly always
  filled, and usually by someone whose own result the paper qualifies.
- **The board.** Board members review or suggest reviewers in their area; the
  board's mix of subfields, methods, and disciplines is the quickest read of
  the pool.
- **Recent authors** of the journal on the same topic or with the same design.
- **A design check** when the identification carries the claim, more often at
  journals with a methods-forward reputation.
- **An outsider** at general journals, to test whether the result matters
  beyond the subfield.
- **Declines make the panel a draw.** Invitations are declined often, and the
  realized two to four reviewers are a sample from the pool, not the editor's
  first choices. That is why the synthesis reports a range over draws.
- **Special issues.** Guest editors invite from their own network; insider
  slots dominate.

## Families

Five types that commonly appear in each family's pool, and what each presses
hardest on. They are slots to instantiate in this paper's terms, not a fixed
panel: swap a type for one the journal's recent volumes show more often.

### General flagships: APSR, AJPS, JOP, BJPS

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Subfield insider | Novelty against the Frontier papers; whether the paper engages the conversation it claims to join |
| Design specialist | The identifying assumption, and the current estimator literature for the design: heterogeneity-robust DiD, weak-IV-robust inference, RD bandwidth and manipulation tests, conjoint estimands |
| Out-of-subfield generalist | Why a political scientist outside the subfield should care; jargon; whether the scope conditions travel |
| Theorist (formal or conceptual) | Whether the hypotheses follow from the mechanism; observable implications beyond the main effect |
| Rival-explanation skeptic | The alternative account, and the test that separates it from the paper's |

APSR weighs broad significance most, so the generalist's report counts heavily.
AJPS verifies replication materials before publication; design scrutiny is the
norm. JOP's short-article format raises the bar on contribution per page.
BJPS is comparative-friendly; its outsider is often a comparativist.

### IR and IPE flagships: IO, ISQ, World Politics

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| OEP / IPE insider | Micro-foundations: whose preferences, aggregated through which institution; firm- and sector-level heterogeneity |
| Quantitative IR methodologist | Dyadic and network dependence, clustering, selection into agreements and treaties, strategic interaction, temporal dependence |
| IR generalist or institutionalist | What the finding means for cooperation, institutions, or order beyond the case; the payoff for IR theory |
| Historical or constructivist skeptic | Concept validity (legitimacy, ideas, norms); process evidence that the mechanism operated; rationalist assumptions doing unstated work |
| Economist | Gravity specification (multilateral resistance, zeros, PPML); the economics literature the paper overlooks; general-equilibrium effects |

IO wants theory plus evidence and desk-rejects often; the generalist asks for
the theoretical payoff. World Politics is theory-heavy and comparative-IR; the
historical skeptic weighs more. ISQ has the broadest IR pool; a security
scholar can be the outsider.

### Heterodox and critical IPE: RIPE, New Political Economy

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Critical or historical IPE scholar | Power, structure, and history; methodological individualism; whether the categories are themselves contested |
| Comparative-capitalism scholar | Varieties of capitalism and growth models; institutional complementarities the analysis flattens |
| Qualitative methodologist | Case selection, within-case and process evidence, what the cross-national data cannot see |
| Quantitative IPE reader | The regressions; usually one reviewer at most in this pool |
| Regional specialist | Global South, EU, or East Asian context; whether the coding fits the place |

Quantitative work is welcome when it engages the journal's debates; a paper
that reads as open-economy politics with no heterodox interlocutor reads as a
misfit.

### Conflict and quantitative IR: JCR, JPR, International Interactions

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Conflict-data specialist | Event coding and reporting bias (UCDP, ACLED, MID); fatality thresholds and aggregation; geocoding precision |
| Quantitative methodologist | Rare events, temporal and spatial dependence, selection on onset, post-treatment controls |
| Bargaining theorist | Information, commitment, and indivisibility; implications beyond the main effect |
| Area or case specialist | Whether the coded cases match what happened |
| Policy-oriented reader | Substantive magnitudes and what they imply for policy |

### Comparative politics: CPS, Comparative Politics, EJPR, WEP, Democratization, SCID

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Country or regional specialist | Case knowledge, measurement fit to context, sources |
| Comparative-institutions theorist | Mechanism and scope conditions across institutional settings |
| Cross-national measurement methodologist | Index construction and equivalence (V-Dem, Polity, survey items across countries); multilevel structure; few clusters |
| Comparative-historical scholar | Sequencing, critical junctures, long-run confounders and persistence |
| Behavior or public-opinion scholar | Micro-level evidence for the macro claim; ecological inference |

### Behavior, public opinion, experiments: Political Behavior, POQ, JEPS, Electoral Studies

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Survey methodologist | Sampling frame, weighting, attention and bot screening, mode and panel effects |
| Experimentalist | Fidelity to the preregistration, manipulation checks, demand effects, multiple comparisons, conjoint estimands |
| Political psychologist | The psychological mechanism: identity, affect, motivated reasoning |
| Domain expert | Whether treatments and outcomes reflect the real-world issue |
| Measurement specialist | Scale validity, dimensionality, ceiling and floor effects |

### Methods: Political Analysis, PSRM

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Statistician or econometrician | Assumptions, proofs, asymptotics, relation to the statistics literature |
| Applied methodologist in the subfield | Whether the problem is real and the fix matters for applied work |
| Simulation skeptic | Monte Carlo design, benchmarks against existing estimators, where the method breaks |
| Software and replication reviewer | Code, package, documentation, scalability |
| Applied user | Whether the application is compelling enough for researchers to adopt the method |

PSRM also publishes substantive research-design papers; draw those panels from
the substantive family.

### Economics-facing: JPubE, JDE, EJ, JEEA, AEJ Applied and Policy, Economics & Politics, World Trade Review

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Applied microeconometrician | Identification, clustering, event-study pre-trends, heterogeneity-robust DiD, inference with few clusters |
| Field economist (trade, development, public) | Novelty against the economics literature; political-science citations do not establish it |
| Theory or structural economist | A model or conceptual framework; welfare and general-equilibrium implications |
| Political-economy economist | The political mechanism in the economics political-economy literature's terms |
| Data and measurement skeptic | Administrative-data construction, measurement error, sample construction |

Economics referees expect a conceptual framework, magnitudes interpreted in
economic units, and engagement with the economics literature; a
political-science framing alone reads as thin.

### Interdisciplinary science: PNAS, Nature Human Behaviour, Science Advances, PLOS ONE

| Type | Presses hardest on |
|---|---|
| Disciplinary insider | Whether political scientists would find it new |
| Adjacent-field scientist | Economics, psychology, sociology, or network science on the same question |
| Statistics and reproducibility reviewer | Open data and code, preregistration, robustness, effect-size reporting |
| Generalist scientist | Broad scientific interest; the claim stated without disciplinary jargon |
| Policy reader | Practical implications and their limits |

PLOS ONE reviews for soundness, not significance, so significance objections
carry no weight there.

### Short formats: Research & Politics, APSR and BJPS letters, JOP short articles

Draw from the parent family. Every reviewer also asks whether the single result
stands alone or is a fragment of a longer paper.

## Reading a journal not listed

1. The board: disciplines, subfields, methods.
2. The methods and paradigms in the last two years of articles.
3. The stated aims and scope.
4. The disciplines its authors come from.

Map to the nearest family and adjust the types to match. When a journal spans
two families (ISQ, PSRM, Economics & Politics), draw from both.
